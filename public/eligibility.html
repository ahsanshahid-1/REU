Eligibility

Who can participate

These rules come directly from NSF's REU solicitation (NSF 23-601, Section IV). If your situation is unusual, ask us before ruling yourself out.

You are eligible if you are:

  • A U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or U.S. permanent resident. NSF requires this for all REU participants supported with NSF funds.
  • Enrolled in a degree program, part time or full time, leading to a bachelor's or associate degree.
  • A community college student. Associate degree students are fully eligible and strongly encouraged to apply.
  • A first year or second year student. NSF specifically encourages involving students at earlier stages of their college experience.
  • Transferring between institutions and enrolled at neither during the intervening summer. The solicitation explicitly permits this.
  • A high school graduate who has been accepted at an undergraduate institution but has not yet started undergraduate study.

You are not eligible if you:

  • Have received your bachelor's degree and are no longer enrolled as an undergraduate.
  • Are an international student without U.S. citizenship, U.S. national status, or permanent residency. This is an NSF wide rule for REU funding, not a choice this Site makes.
Non-discrimination. Per PAPPG Chapter XI.A and the solicitation, race, ethnicity, sex, age, and disability status are never used as eligibility criteria. Our goal, in NSF's words, is to achieve a diverse pool of applicants and then consider all eligible applicants in that pool.
Students with disabilities. We provide accommodations for research and residential life, and NSF's FASED mechanism can fund special assistance or equipment. Contact us early so arrangements are ready before you arrive.

Recruitment and selection

Who we recruit, and how selection works

The solicitation makes recruitment a formal review criterion for REU Sites. These are our public commitments:

At least half from limited-research institutions

At least half of participants are recruited from academic institutions where research opportunities in STEM are limited, including two year colleges, as the solicitation requires of every Site.

A significant fraction from outside the host

This is a national program: a significant fraction of participants must come from outside UA Little Rock, and most of the cohort does.

Comprehensive outreach

We conduct outreach, awareness, and recruitment efforts encouraging students representing the full spectrum of diverse talent in STEM to apply, through community college partners, minority serving institutions, EPSCoR networks, and student organizations.

How selection works. A faculty committee reviews every complete application against your personal statement, preparation relative to opportunity, references, and fit with the project areas. Selection is done in compliance with non-discrimination statutes and regulations (PAPPG Chapter XI.A). Applications are received through this website; NSF's Education & Training Application (ETAP) is also supported, and both routes receive identical consideration.
Evaluation and reporting

How the program is evaluated, and what NSF asks of you

The solicitation requires a plan to measure the project's success qualitatively and quantitatively. In the interest of transparency, here is what participation involves on the reporting side.

  • An external evaluation with pre and post program measures of student learning and perspectives on research, following the solicitation's guidance.
  • Structured tracking beyond graduation, which the solicitation calls highly desirable, to gauge the lasting influence of the experience on students' career paths.
  • Each participant is identified to NSF as an "REU Participant" in annual project reports, with home institution and year of schooling completed, as required.
  • Participants receive an automated request from Research.gov to self-report demographic information. Responding is voluntary.
  • After the program begins, this website expands to showcase the cohort: participants, projects, symposium presentations, publications, and outcomes, which also feeds the "Results from Prior NSF Support" record for future renewals.
