Nature of student activities

What you will actually do

All six project areas share a common intellectual focus — data-driven discovery and emerging technologies, connecting nanoscale materials research with data science, visualization, and secure computing. That shared theme is what lets ten students from different projects learn from one another as a single cohort, in the weekly seminar and across the summer.

Each applicant ranks the project areas below; we match you with a mentor whose active work fits your interests.

Detailed example projects for each area appear here once the cohort's mentor list is finalized. Published upon NSF award notification.

Nanotechnology and advanced materials

Synthesize and characterize nanoscale materials in the Center for Integrative Nanotechnology Sciences, using electron microscopy, scanning probe microscopy, X-ray diffraction, and optical spectroscopy. Projects address multifunctional materials with applications from energy to sensing.

Nanomedicine and bioengineering

Work on biomedical applications of nanoscience, including bone and tissue regeneration materials, in collaboration with partners such as the University of Arkansas for Medical Sciences. Students learn how materials research translates toward clinical impact.

Data science and analytics

Turn large, messy, real-world datasets from industry, government, and research partners into defensible conclusions. Projects cover statistical modeling, machine learning, and the honest evaluation of data-driven claims.

Immersive visualization and extended reality

Build and study virtual, augmented, and mixed reality systems in the Emerging Analytics Center, home to a CAVE immersive environment. Projects apply interactive visualization to complex, big-data problems.

Cybersecurity and resilient computing

Investigate how to secure networks, data, and mission-critical systems against evolving threats, one of the university's fastest growing research strengths. Projects range from applied security analysis to trustworthy machine learning.

Applied mathematics and computational modeling

Develop the mathematical and computational models that underpin the other five areas, from simulations of physical systems to the statistics of experimental design. Ideal for students from mathematics and physics backgrounds.

Week by week

  • Week 1. Orientation covering program expectations and the code of conduct, training in the responsible and ethical conduct of research, and a skills bootcamp so every student starts from a common baseline.
  • Weeks 2 to 8. Full time research with your mentor and group; weekly cohort seminar; professional development workshops on graduate school, careers, and scientific communication.
  • Week 9. Writing workshop, poster and talk preparation, practice presentations with feedback.
  • Week 10. Final research symposium where every student presents, followed by planning for continued collaboration through the academic year.

After the summer

  • Mentors continue interacting with students during the academic year, to the extent practicable, to connect the research experience to your overall course of study, as the solicitation directs.
  • Conference travel support to present your results at a regional or national venue.
  • Recommendation letters and graduate school application guidance from your mentor.
  • Support toward publication when summer results warrant it.
The research environment

Where the work happens

UA Little Rock is a metropolitan public research university classified as an R2 doctoral university with high research activity. Research takes place across the Donaghey College of Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics and the university's more than twenty research centers, including two purpose-built facilities students in this program use directly.

  • Faculty mentors. Mentors are drawn from faculty across the Donaghey College of Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics and the university's research centers, selected for expertise and a documented history of involving undergraduates in research, including publishing with undergraduate coauthors.
  • Mentor training. All mentors complete structured training before the summer, covering research supervision and professional conduct, as the solicitation requires.
  • Sustained mentoring. Mentoring relationships continue after students leave the Site, through academic year check-ins, publication support, and recommendation letters.

Named mentor profiles, with research descriptions and undergraduate mentoring records, appear here once the award is finalized, so applicants can see exactly who they would work with. Published upon NSF award notification.

Professional development and conduct

Development, and a safe, respectful, inclusive environment

The solicitation requires every REU Site to plan for four things in this area. Here is how this Site meets each one.

  • (1) Student professional development, including training in the responsible and ethical conduct of research, plus workshops on scientific writing, presentation, graduate school, and careers.
  • (2) Mentor selection: research mentors are chosen for expertise and a track record of mentoring undergraduates, documented in the mentor roster above.
  • (3) Mentor training and monitoring: structured training before the summer and program-level check-ins during it, so mentoring quality is supervised, not assumed.
  • (4) Communicating expectations of behavior to ensure a safe, respectful, inclusive, harassment-free environment for all participants.

Code of conduct and harassment policy

NSF does not tolerate sexual harassment, or any other form of harassment, where NSF funded activities take place, and neither do we.

  • All participants in the Site (REU students, faculty, postdocs, graduate students, and other research mentors) attend an orientation covering expectations of behavior.
  • The orientation reviews the university's policy addressing sexual harassment, other forms of harassment, and sexual assault, including reporting and complaint procedures.
  • For any off campus or off site research, the university certifies a plan for a safe and inclusive working environment, per PAPPG Chapter II.E.9.
