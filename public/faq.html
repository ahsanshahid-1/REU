Applying

What the application asks for

  • A free applicant account with a verified email, so you can track your application status.
  • Contact details and confirmation of NSF eligibility.
  • Your institution, degree program, and academic year, so we can honor our recruitment commitments.
  • A ranked choice of the project areas above.
  • A personal statement of 300 to 600 words. We read for curiosity, not polish.
  • An unofficial transcript (PDF). There is no GPA cutoff.
  • Names and emails of two references; we contact them directly, so there are no letters to chase.

FAQ

Questions students actually ask

I've never done research. Should I still apply?

Yes, emphatically. NSF encourages REU Sites to involve students at earlier stages, before they have committed to a research path, and our selection process is designed around potential, not prior output.

Is there a GPA cutoff?

No. We ask for a transcript to understand your preparation, not to filter by a number. Your personal statement and references carry more weight.

I attend a community college. Am I really eligible?

Yes. Students in associate degree programs are fully eligible under the solicitation, and at least half of our cohort must come from institutions where STEM research opportunities are limited, including two year colleges.

I'm transferring schools this summer. Does that disqualify me?

No. The solicitation explicitly permits students transferring from one institution to another who are enrolled at neither during the intervening summer.

I'm an international student. Can I participate?

Unfortunately no, unless you are a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, or U.S. permanent resident. This is an NSF wide requirement that applies to every REU Site in the country.

Is the stipend a salary? Will I be an employee?

No. The solicitation defines the REU experience as a research training experience paid via a stipend, not employment paid with a salary or wage. The university may issue payments through its normal payroll system for administrative convenience, but that does not change the nature of the experience.

Stipend funds may be taxable income; NSF points students to the IRS and its Tax Benefits for Education page.

Can I get academic credit for the summer?

Optionally, yes. The solicitation permits Sites to offer credit as an option; it is never required, and you are never charged tuition to participate.

What if I need accommodations for a disability?

Tell us as early as you are comfortable, and we will arrange accommodations for research and residential life. NSF's Facilitation Awards for Scientists and Engineers with Disabilities (FASED) can additionally fund special assistance or equipment for work on NSF supported projects.

Are teachers ever involved?

NSF encourages research experiences for K-12 STEM teachers coordinated with REU projects (the RET mechanism). Interested teachers should contact the program directly.

Where can I find other REU programs?

NSF maintains a national directory of active REU Sites across all fields at nsf.gov/crssprgm/reu. Applying to several programs is normal and encouraged.
